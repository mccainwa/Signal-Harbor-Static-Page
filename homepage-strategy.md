# Signal Harbor — Homepage Strategy

*AI Visibility Audit for local businesses. Domain: signalharborconsulting.com*

This document defines the homepage strategy: navigation, a section-by-section wireframe, headline and CTA options, draft copy, design direction, the asset list, and a recommended build order. Placeholders are marked in brackets where I don't have real data, per the project's accuracy rules — nothing here invents customers, results, or proprietary claims.

---

## 1. Recommended Navigation

Keep the top bar to six items plus one button. More than that dilutes the primary action.

**Primary nav (left to right):**

`Signal Harbor [logo]` — `AI Visibility Audit` — `How It Works` — `Use Cases` — `Sample Report` — `About` — **`Book an Audit`** (button, electric blue)

Rationale:

- **AI Visibility Audit** is the product page (what you get, the framework, scoring). It earns the first slot because it's the offer.
- **How It Works** covers process and methodology for skeptics who need to understand the mechanism.
- **Use Cases** holds the vertical-specific pages (PI law, construction software). Use a dropdown only once you have 2+ verticals live; until then link it straight to the primary vertical.
- **Sample Report** is a soft-conversion path for people not ready to book — it doubles as proof.
- **About** carries founder credibility, which matters for an early-stage, founder-led consultancy.
- **Contact** lives in the footer, not the top nav, to keep the bar uncluttered.
- The **Book an Audit** button is the only colored element in the nav so the eye lands on it.

**Footer nav** (fuller): Product (Audit, How It Works, Sample Report), Use Cases (PI Law, Construction), Company (About, Contact), plus legal (Privacy, Terms) and the email/booking link.

Sticky behavior: nav sticks on scroll with the Book an Audit button always visible.

## 3. Hero Headline Options

Each pairs an H1 with a subhead. All avoid hype and state a concrete idea.

**Option A — Direct problem statement (recommended)**
H1: **When people ask AI for a recommendation, does your business come up?**
Subhead: Search is shifting from links to answers. Signal Harbor tests whether ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot, and Grok mention, cite, or recommend your business — and shows you exactly where you're invisible.

**Option B — The blind-spot angle**
H1: **You rank on Google. You may still be invisible inside AI answers.**
Subhead: Most local businesses have no idea how AI systems describe, cite, or rank them. We measure it, score it 0–100, and flag what AI gets wrong.

**Option C — The stakes**
H1: **AI is already recommending businesses like yours. The question is whether it's recommending you.**
Subhead: Signal Harbor audits how ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot, and Grok see your business — mentions, citations, competitor share of voice, and hallucinations.

**Option D — Category-defining**
H1: **AI visibility is the new search visibility. Find out where you stand.**
Subhead: Get an AI Visibility Audit that scores your business 0–100 and shows whether AI systems cite you, skip you, or get your details wrong.

My pick: **Option A** for the homepage — it's a question the buyer immediately wants answered, names the systems concretely, and avoids category jargon before the value lands. Hold B in reserve for the Problem section or PI-law landing page.

**Eyebrow label** (small text above H1): `AI VISIBILITY AUDIT` or `AI VISIBILITY INTELLIGENCE`

## 5. Section Copy (draft)

Tight, specific, no hype. Edit freely.

**Hero** — see Option A above.

**Problem**
Title: **Search is moving from links to answers.**
Intro: For twenty years, being found meant ranking on Google. That's changing. People now ask AI systems for recommendations directly — and those systems answer with a short list, not ten blue links.

- Card 1 — *The shift is already here.* Buyers ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Copilot and Grok "who's the best [service] near me?" and act on the answer.
- Card 2 — *Ranking isn't the same as being recommended.* You can sit at the top of Google and still never appear in an AI-generated answer. Different systems, different rules.
- Card 3 — *AI can get you wrong.* It may recommend your competitors, cite outdated pages, or state incorrect details about your business as fact.

**Solution**
Title: **We test how AI systems actually see your business.**
Intro: Signal Harbor runs the prompts your customers would ask and records what each AI system says back — whether you're mentioned, whether you're cited, who's winning the recommendation, and where the answers are wrong.

- Tests how AI systems describe and rank you
- Measures how often you're mentioned and cited
- Compares your share of voice against competitors
- Flags hallucinations — incorrect claims AI states as fact

**Audit Framework**
Title: **Six factors that decide whether AI recommends you.**
Intro: Your audit scores each factor and rolls them into a single 0–100 score.

- **Prompt Visibility** — Do you appear when customers ask AI for recommendations in your category, market, and service area?
- **Technical Access** — Can AI systems and crawlers reach, read, and interpret the pages that matter?
- **Entity Clarity** — Is it clear who you are, what you do, where you operate, and which customers you serve?
- **Content Extractability** — Can AI systems pull accurate, useful answers from your website and public profiles?
- **Authority Signals** — Do trusted sources consistently confirm your business details, expertise, and reputation?
- **Hallucination Detection** — Where are AI systems stating incorrect, outdated, or unsupported information as fact?

**Metrics Dashboard Preview**
Title: **One score. The metrics behind it. The errors to fix.**
Intro: Your latest audit score sits front and center, backed by the metrics that explain it and a clear flag on anything AI gets wrong.

- **Mention Rate** — How often your business appears across tested prompts.
- **Citation Share** — How often AI systems cite your site or trusted third-party sources about you.
- **Share of Voice** — Your visibility compared with competitors in the same answers.
- **Prominence Score** — How strongly and prominently your business is positioned when it appears.
- **Hallucination Detection** — Incorrect or outdated claims flagged separately for review.

**Case Study / Proof of Concept**
Title: **Proof of concept: how the automated audit compares to a manual one.**
Body: We ran the Signal Harbor audit against a manual review of a construction-software example and compared the findings. `[Insert summary of what the automated audit caught vs. the manual audit]`. Metrics: `[Insert audit score]`, `[Insert mention rate]`, `[Insert hallucination example]`.
*Note: these are placeholders. No numbers should ship here until you provide real findings.*

**Differentiation**
Title: **This is not SEO.**
Intro: SEO optimizes how you rank on a results page. AI visibility is about how AI systems retrieve, summarize, cite, and recommend you in an answer. Related, but not the same — and most SEO work doesn't touch it.

| | Traditional SEO | Signal Harbor (AI Visibility) |
|---|---|---|
| Optimizes for | Search engine rankings | How AI systems cite and recommend you |
| You show up as | A link on a results page | A named recommendation inside an answer |
| Measures | Keywords, rankings, backlinks, traffic | Mentions, citations, share of voice, hallucinations |
| "Winning" means | Page-one ranking | Being recommended and accurately described by AI |
| Common blind spot | Assumes ranking = visibility | Catches what AI says when no one's checking |

**Final CTA**
Headline: **Find out what AI says about your business.**
Sub: Get a 0–100 AI Visibility Audit across six factors — and see exactly where you're invisible, miscited, or misrepresented.
Buttons: `Book an Audit` · `View Sample Report`

## 6. Visual Design Recommendations

**Palette**
Navy `#0A1628` (authority sections, hero, footer), Electric blue `#00C2FF` (accents, primary buttons, key data points only — use sparingly so it stays high-impact), white and light gray for readable content sections, plus a dark muted tone for secondary panels. Add a restrained alert color (amber/red) used *only* for hallucination flags so that signal stays meaningful.

**Type**
Sora for headings (tight tracking on large H1s), DM Sans for body. Establish a clear scale: oversized H1, distinctly smaller H2 section titles, generous body line-height. Lean on size and weight contrast for hierarchy rather than color.

**Layout & rhythm**
Alternate dark and light section backgrounds down the page. Generous vertical spacing between sections. Card-based blocks for the problem, framework, and metrics. Keep line length readable in light sections (~65–75 characters).

**The hero**
Dark navy, subtle premium gradient acceptable (navy to slightly lighter navy — avoid rainbow gradients). Pair the headline with a clean product mockup: the audit score as a 0–100 gauge/dial with a couple of metric chips. This makes the abstract concept (AI visibility) instantly concrete.

**The dashboard preview (centerpiece)**
This is the most important visual on the page. Make the **audit score dominant** — large dial or number, electric-blue accent. Surround it with metric tiles (Mention Rate, Citation Share, Share of Voice, Prominence). Give **Hallucination Alerts** its own clearly-flagged panel with the alert color. Show latest score primary; history as a small sparkline or "View history" link, never a cluttered table by default.

**Icons & imagery**
Use a single thin-line icon set, sparingly — one per framework card, nothing decorative. No childish illustrations, no generic stock photos of people pointing at laptops. Favor data-style visuals: dials, bars, comparison panels.

**Buttons**
Primary = solid electric blue on navy, or navy on light. Secondary = outline/ghost. Consistent radius. Clear hover and focus states.

**Accessibility**
Check contrast — electric blue on navy can fail for small body text, so reserve blue for large text, accents, and buttons, not paragraph copy. Use semantic headings (one H1, ordered H2/H3). Visible focus rings. Buttons large enough for touch. Don't rely on color alone for hallucination flags — pair with an icon and label.

**Responsive**
Mobile: collapse multi-card rows to single column, keep the score dial large, keep a CTA reachable (sticky bottom bar or sticky nav button). Comparison table becomes stacked rows on small screens.

## 8. What to Build First

Sequence to get a credible, conversion-ready page up fast, then deepen.

**Phase 1 — Conversion skeleton (build now)**
Stand up the homepage with Hero, Problem, Solution, Audit Framework, Final CTA, and footer — using the draft copy above. Wire both CTAs to a booking link (Calendly or similar) and a Sample Report placeholder. This alone can start booking audits. Lock the nav and brand tokens here.

**Phase 2 — The proof layer**
Build the Metrics Dashboard Preview (the centerpiece visual) and the Differentiation comparison. These convert skeptics. Replace placeholder mockups with real ones as soon as the dashboard design exists.

**Phase 3 — Credibility & depth**
Add the Case Study section with **real** audit data, the Sample Report itself, the AI Visibility Audit product page, How It Works, and About. Swap every placeholder for real content.

**Phase 4 — Vertical landing pages**
Build dedicated pages for the priority verticals (Chicago PI law first, then construction software), reusing the homepage components with vertical-specific headlines and examples. These become your paid-traffic and outbound destinations.

**Build-order principle:** ship the page that can book an audit first (Phase 1), then layer proof and depth. Don't block launch on assets you don't have yet — use placeholders, go live, and replace.
